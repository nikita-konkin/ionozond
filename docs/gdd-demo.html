GDD from one 1 MHz window: delay slope against phase fit

A simulated stretch of a Cyprus sweep, 1 MHz wide, which is 10 s at 100 kHz/s. The same window is turned into GDD two ways: a straight line through each column's delay, and a parabola through each column's phase. Fading makes the echo's phase wander over those 10 s, and the parabola reads that wander as dispersion. Background: Oblique Chirp Ionograms, Part 11.

Error distributions of both methods.

The model is deliberately small. Fading is a sum of 40 sinusoids with Gaussian-distributed Doppler frequencies. A column's delay error is its mean Doppler shift (10 µs per Hz at 100 kHz/s) plus noise of 60·(0.1 s / column)/√SNR µs, which matches full FFT processing of synthetic captures. Its phase is the fading phase at the column centre plus noise, unwrapped across the columns that pass 6 dB. Absolute numbers are a guide. Full processing of synthetic captures gave, at 0.37 Hz and 18 dB: delay slope ±3.6 µs/MHz at 0.41 s and ±4.4 at 0.1 s, phase fit ±6.7 and ±5.9.
